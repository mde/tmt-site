Tinnitus apps presenting as therapy or treatment

US App Store listings, checked October 10, 2026

Apple rejected Tinnitus Multi-Therapy under App Review Guideline 1.4.1, citing the need for regulatory clearance and stating that “your app name implies therapy.” The 64 apps below were available on the US App Store when checked. Each presents itself as therapy or treatment for tinnitus in its name, subtitle or description.

No regulatory clearance for any of these apps was found in their App Store listings, on the developer websites that could be reached, or in FDA 510(k) searches for tinnitus devices. That can’t rule out documentation a developer may have given Apple privately.

Every listing was read in full. Each quote is copied exactly from the listing, and “…” marks omitted text.

What each tag means
Therapy, treatment or cure in the name
The app’s name on the App Store contains therapy, treatment or cure.
Therapy or treatment in the subtitle
The subtitle shown under the app’s name on the App Store contains therapy or treatment.
Calls itself therapy or treatment
The description calls the app, or a feature of it, therapy or treatment.
Claims to reduce tinnitus or its impact
The listing says the app makes tinnitus quieter, less noticeable or less bothersome, or reduces its symptoms or effects. A general promise of “relief” does not count on its own.
Cites research or clinical evidence
The description points to research, studies, clinical guidelines or scientific evidence for its approach. This is a claim about the method, not evidence that the app itself was tested.
No medical disclaimer in listing
The App Store listing never says the app is not a medical device or not a substitute for medical care, and never advises seeing a doctor or audiologist. Disclaimers on a developer’s website or inside the app are not counted.
Medical category
The app is listed in the App Store’s Medical category.
